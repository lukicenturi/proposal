Process

Hand-picked at every step

From plantation to storage, in six stages. We pick at full maturity, cure slowly and test every lot, and at each stage we tell you what we measure, so you know what stands behind a lot before it reaches your incoming inspection.

01

Stage 1: Plantation

Our vanilla vines and cocoa trees grow on our plantation and with partner farmer groups in Indonesia, the only major vanilla origin that harvests and cures all year. Supply never waits on a single season.

Every lot is traceable to the farmer group that grew it, the traceability large buyers now write into their sourcing policy.

What we check

  • Farmer-group registration
  • Agronomic practice
  • Pesticide use records

02

Stage 2: Harvest

We hand-pick vanilla beans only at full maturity. Immature, early-picked beans are the best-known quality problem in Indonesian vanilla and a main reason buyers moved away from the origin, so we never pick or buy them.

Cocoa pods are cut by hand when ripe, then opened for fermentation.

What we check

  • Maturity at picking
  • Bean length at harvest
  • Pod ripeness

03

Stage 3: Drying and curing

We cure vanilla slowly, over weeks, to its target moisture: 25 to 33 percent for gourmet grades, 18 to 25 percent for extraction grade. Uneven curing and high moisture are where mould starts, so we measure curing rather than judge it by eye.

Cocoa is fermented, then sun-dried in covered drying houses that keep rain off the beans.

What we check

  • Moisture
  • Water activity
  • Mould
  • Even curing

04

Stage 4: Quality control and grading

We grade beans by hand, by length and appearance, to our published standard. Then we sample each lot and send it to an accredited laboratory, for example Saraswanti or SIG in Indonesia, or an international laboratory such as SGS or Eurofins, for a certificate of analysis.

A lot that fails is sold as extraction grade or rejected to the supplier. It never ships as Grade A. Our full-time QC manager in Indonesia oversees grading and testing.

What we check

  • Vanillin by HPLC
  • Moisture
  • Water activity
  • Mould and yeast
  • Aflatoxin
  • Heavy metals
  • Pesticide multi-residue

Passes: ships as graded, with its certificate of analysis.

Fails: sold as extraction grade or rejected, never shipped as Grade A.

05

Stage 5: Packaging

We re-grade approved lots and vacuum-pack them in 1 kg and 5 kg food-grade pouches inside lined cartons. Every pack carries its origin, harvest window and lot number.

Retail packs: 2, 5 or 10 beans in glass tubes or pouches, and 250 g and 1 kg bags.

What we check

  • Seal integrity
  • Lot label
  • Carton count

06

Stage 6: Storage

We hold beans at 15 to 20 °C and controlled humidity at every stage, from the curing house until the day they are loaded for export.

Cartons are stored and rotated by lot number, so the oldest lot ships first and every carton still matches its certificate.

What we check

  • Temperature and humidity logs
  • Rotation by lot, first in, first out
